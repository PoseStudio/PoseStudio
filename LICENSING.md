# Licensing PoseStudio

PoseStudio is free and open-source software. This page explains what its license lets you do,
what the license asks of you in return, and how to get a commercial license when the
open-source terms don't fit your plans.

This page is a summary. The license texts are what count: [LICENSE](LICENSE) and
[LICENSE-ADDITIONAL-TERMS.txt](LICENSE-ADDITIONAL-TERMS.txt).

## The open-source license

PoseStudio is licensed under the **GNU Affero General Public License, version 3** (AGPL v3).
Its copyright holders add three terms under section 7 of that license, plus a permission for
NVIDIA's render software. Both are in [LICENSE-ADDITIONAL-TERMS.txt](LICENSE-ADDITIONAL-TERMS.txt).

Versions up to and including 0.4.4 were released under the GNU General Public License,
version 3, and remain available under that license.

## What you can do

- **Use PoseStudio for anything**, commercial work included. Pose, light and render, then sell
  the images, animations and poses you make. The license covers PoseStudio itself, not your
  scenes, renders or content.
- **Study and change the source code.**
- **Share copies**, modified or not, as long as you meet the conditions below.

## What the license asks in return

These conditions apply if you distribute PoseStudio or any program that contains part of it.
They also apply if you let people use a modified version over a network.

- **Publish the source.** Release the complete source code of the whole program under the AGPL
  v3, including your own changes and additions.
- **Keep the attribution.** Keep the copyright and license notices. Keep the line *"PoseStudio by
  Ion Hatzithomas and the PoseStudio contributors - https://posestudio.org"* in the legal notices
  the program displays (Help → License in PoseStudio).
- **Use a name of your own.** Mark a modified version as different from PoseStudio, and don't
  present it as PoseStudio or as endorsed by its authors.
- **Respect the trademarks.** Use the PoseStudio name and logo only as the
  [trademark policy](TRADEMARKS.md) allows.

## Commercial licenses

If the AGPL's conditions don't work for you, a commercial license lets you use PoseStudio's
code without them. You need one if you want to:

- build PoseStudio, or part of it, into a product whose source you don't publish. That covers a
  closed application, a plugin or an engine, and parts such as the posing and IK system, the
  importers or the render engine bridge;
- offer a modified PoseStudio, or software built from its code, as an online service without
  publishing the source;
- distribute PoseStudio under terms the AGPL doesn't allow;
- get terms the open-source license doesn't include, such as a warranty or support.

Commercial licenses are granted by PoseStudio's maintainer, Ion Hatzithomas, or an organization
he forms to steward the project. To ask, email **community@posestudio.org** and say what you
plan to build. Terms are agreed case by case.

A commercial license covers PoseStudio's own code. The third-party components it builds on
stay under their own licenses (see [ATTRIBUTIONS.md](ATTRIBUTIONS.md)). For example, Qt remains
under the LGPL v3 unless you license Qt commercially from The Qt Company, and NVIDIA's software
remains under NVIDIA's terms.

## Contributions

Contributors sign the [PoseStudio Contributor License Agreement](.github/CONTRIBUTING.md#contributor-license-agreement-cla).
They keep the copyright to their work. The agreement gives the project the right to offer that
work under both the open-source license and commercial licenses. The income from commercial
licenses helps fund PoseStudio's development.
