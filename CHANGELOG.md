# Changelog

All notable changes to the PoseStudio project will be documented in this file.

The format is based on [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.0.0/),
and this project adheres to [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/spec/v2.0.0.html).

> **Note on early release numbering:** the commits for two of the releases below were pushed
> with mislabeled titles — `ba4033f` ("Release 0.4.0") and `83cb038` ("Release 0.5.0") are the
> releases recorded here as **[0.3.3]** and **[0.3.4]**. Commit messages can't be rewritten on a
> public repository; this changelog and `CMakeLists.txt` (whose `project(VERSION)` stamps
> `Constants::APP_VERSION` at build time) are the authoritative version record, and releases are tagged from 0.3.0 onward.

## [Unreleased]

### Added
* **Lifted off the floor, she hangs.** A figure pulled up past her reach by a hand now dangles from it as a body does — the legs come together and hang straight down with the knees soft and the feet pointed, and the free arm hangs at her side — instead of keeping her standing stance in mid-air. Lower her and she lands and stands back up as before.
* **A hand on the hip stands akimbo.** Bringing a hand to rest on the hip now takes the elbow out to the side as a person's does, instead of leaving the arm hanging straight with the hand pressed to the hip. Drag the elbow afterwards for a straighter arm.
* **Idle arms swing with a stride.** Arms hanging at her sides now swing against the legs as the feet step apart or a foot is slid forward, as a walker's do, the leading arm's elbow bending a little, and come back as the stance closes; the swing fades as the hips come down, so a lunge keeps its arms at her sides. Posed arms are left alone. Walking frames no longer hang their arms straight down like a mannequin's.
* **Move several joints together.** `Shift` + click adds a joint to the selection (and takes it out again); `Shift` + drag any of the selected joints and they all move together, by the same amount, through the one you pressed. The body answers as it does to a single joint — planted feet, balance, steps, a fold or a crouch under a push, tiptoe and lift-off under a pull — so both hands can be raised, pushed forward or brought down at once, or a hand and a foot placed together. Fingers and toes stay posed on their own.

### Changed
* **The posing engine, refactored inside — same poses, to the last bit.** The full-body-IK code was read end to end and cleaned up: its longest functions split into named parts, every rule that had been copied written once, dead code removed, and its hot paths tightened (the solver reuses its working memory and factors only the rows in play; the rig caches what it used to re-derive every tick). Every step was verified against the previous build on all eight reference figures with identical output, and the in-app gallery and the 23-character sweep read as before. Nothing changes for the user beyond a lighter drag tick. The posing test harness had the same treatment: its one 5,000-line phase function is a class of sixteen phase families, with its printed reports byte-identical before and after.
* **Ctrl + drag on the torso bends the torso alone.** Ctrl + dragging the chest, the upper chest or the belly bends the spine from the grabbed joint down to the pelvis and stops there — the pelvis and the legs stay exactly where they are, the arms and the head ride the chest — so the upper body can be leaned, twisted or arched on a stance already set. (Since 0.3.16 a Ctrl + drag anywhere on the body moved the whole figure; that is now the hips' gesture alone — the lower belly included, which the rig had treated as part of the hips, and the collar bones and the base of the neck, whose own joints a drag could not move: a Ctrl + drag anywhere on the upper chest or the throat bends the torso.) A pinned hand below the chain holds, its arm folding or straightening to keep it there.
* **Interface text is ready for translation.** Every menu, dialog, tooltip and message in the app is now marked as translatable, so other languages can be added later without reworking the code. Nothing changes on screen: PoseStudio is English-only for now. The shading and view picker names are marked for the translation tools too, so a future catalog cannot lose them. (#33, #38 — thanks @DmitryAkDev)

### Fixed
* **Lying down on her belly works on every body.** From all fours, dragging the hips forward and down lays the figure out flat — the back straightens, the hips rest on the thighs and the belly, the face comes down, and the hands let go and lie along her sides — on heavy and long-armed characters as on the base figures. Before, the arms stood as struts under the chest on such bodies, the hips hung 20cm in the air and the pelvis tipped past flat. The idle arms also keep clear of the floor and the hips more smoothly as she sits down, reclines or lies back.
* **A click anywhere on the hand selects the hand.** The small bones inside the hand (and the foot's ball, heel and mid-foot) were selectable joints of their own, so a click on the palm as often took one of them as the hand, and the highlight showed only a sliver of it. They are the hand's own flesh now: a click on them selects the hand, which lights up whole, as a click on the face selects the head.
* **A seated figure reaching with a hand hinges at the hips.** With the hips pinned to a seat, the pelvis rocked only when the chest or head was dragged; reaching with a hand bent the spine over an upright pelvis. A hand reach now rocks the pelvis as a trunk drag does, so a hand brought to the knee or reached forward leans her from the hips.
* **Getting up off the belly works on bodies that lie flatter than the base figure.** On such a body the mid-thigh helper bone touched the floor before the knee did and was treated as the knee: dragging the hips up then lifted the thigh about its middle, and she arched into a cobra instead of coming up onto all fours (on a heavy build a toe was flung half a metre). Helper bones are no longer floor contacts; the knee is. Two more things on heavy bodies: a knee resting on the floor no longer floats up when a hand is dragged, and a knee is still a floor contact while the belly rests on the floor too.
* **Arms kept off the floor move smoothly as she lies down.** Laid down on her back or sat down onto the floor, her idle arms are turned just clear of the floor; that turn was chosen from sixteen fixed directions, so as the body went down the arms hopped between two of them, four degrees in one frame — a visible twitch of the hands, worst on long-armed characters, that could land in a still hold. The clearing direction is found exactly now and the arms follow it continuously, kept a little further from the floor so that a hand that merely hangs beside her is never taken for a hand she is leaning on.
* **Standing up by the hips ends upright, looking ahead.** Pulled up to standing by the hips from all fours or out of a sit-up, she rose with her trunk still folded and her gaze on the floor: the pelvis came upright with the rise, but the spine kept the fold the drag began in and the head the hang it had. The trunk now unfolds as she rises and the head comes up to look ahead. And hands that were on the floor let go cleanly as the shoulders rise beyond the arms' reach: their fading hold had been curling the spine against its rest, which sprang back in the tick they let go — up to 34cm at a fingertip in one frame. And from an all-fours whose feet lie a thigh's length behind her, the hips pulled up stand her up by stepping her feet forward, where she used to stop with her feet trailing.
* **A knee drawn up out of a kneel on a heavy body no longer flings the foot.** The lifted foot of a half kneel was asked to hang straight under the knee while it still pointed down, where its toes could not be; it waited behind, held by the floor, and swung under in one frame once the foot levelled, 16-20cm at a toe on larger figures. The foot's target now waits for a foot the floor is holding back and leads it on at a steady pace once it can follow.
* **She stands up off her knees.** Pulling the hips up from a kneel sat back on the heels raised her to a tall kneel and no further: the knees stayed planted however far the cursor went, because a knee the hips were low over when the drag began was held down for the whole drag (the rule that brings her up onto all fours off her belly). The knees now let go once the hips are asked above a tall kneel, the feet roll back onto their toes and the legs straighten over them, from the heels as from a tall kneel; a knee keeps its footing until its foot has rolled over, so nothing jumps on the way (a fast yank out of a kneel snapped a knee 37cm in one frame before). Getting up off the belly still ends on all fours.
* **A hand that lets go of the floor no longer throws the arm.** Pushing up off the belly, kneeling up off all fours, standing up from a crouch with a hand on the floor: once the arm pulling on a planted hand came straight, the hand's hold went from everything to nothing in one solve, and the arm the hold had stretched to its limits snapped back toward where the drag began — up to 22cm at a thumb in a single frame. The hold now fades over about half a second as the arm straightens, the arm is left where the body took it, and once the hand lifts it hangs from the shoulder, the elbow straightening and the fingers relaxing, instead of folding back to the bend it had on the floor. Getting up off the belly with the hands under the shoulders ends on all fours with no snap. And a drag let go of while an arm is still swinging into its hang, or the head still coming up, now finishes that motion after the mouse-up instead of freezing mid-swing.
* **Joints turn in their authored rotation order.** Each joint's three rotations were composed in the reverse of the order the figure file states. One rotation alone was unaffected, so elbows and knees always checked out, but two together were not: a thigh bent forward and then moved Side-Side rolled about its own length instead of moving out to the side, and on the figure generations whose forearm carries its own twist, a bent forearm's twist swung it round the upper arm. Every joint of every generation now composes as the figure authors it — by the dials, the keys and a drag alike. The posing that had grown around the old composition was re-set with it: a kneel, a crouch and a sit keep the knees over the feet (a thigh that truly abducts had found a frog's way down, both knees splayed out), and a figure sat down onto the floor keeps her hands off it instead of folding forward over them. **Poses saved with earlier versions load slightly differently** at joints with two or three rotations set: the numbers in the file are the same, composed the right way round now.
* **Skin textures on characters that mix UV layouts.** Each material zone of a figure now reads its maps through the UV layout its material names, instead of the geometry's default layout for every zone. A newer-generation character commonly keeps the previous generation's skin maps and names that generation's layout for the skin while its eyes and nails use the new one; read through the default alone, every skin feature landed slightly off — a crack-like seam along the scalp, a patch of mismatched skin between the shoulder blades, the notch of the throat displaced. Figures on a single layout render exactly as before.
* **Eyelashes no longer render as grey strips on the newest figure generation.** That generation's eyelash follower carries only a placeholder material in its own file; its real materials — the cutout mask, the normal map, the dark colour — live in a hierarchical materials preset the character lists under its *tear* follower, and the importer applied each follower's presets to that follower alone. A hierarchical preset addresses each follower by name, so it is now applied to every follower it names, whichever slot lists it. The base figure of that generation and the characters built on it get their lashes; figures whose presets sit under the eyelash slot, the previous generation's integral lashes and stylized characters with per-follower presets render exactly as before.
* **Manual contents highlight.** In Help → User Manual, the highlighted contents entry could land on whichever row sat under a resting mouse pointer after the tree re-laid out, instead of the page that was opened; it now stays on the opened entry. (#37 — thanks @DmitryAkDev)

## [0.3.16] - 2026-09-30

Project files and joint-by-joint posing. The whole scene — every figure with its pose, the
lighting and the camera — saves and opens as a `.pss` project (File → New / Open / Save, with an
unsaved-changes prompt and the project's name in the title bar — contributed by @DmitryAkDev). A joint is turned on its own two new ways: the **Transform** tab's Bend /
Side-Side / Twist dials, and the `B` / `S` / `T` keys that turn it with the mouse the way Blender
does — the X/Y/Z wheel is gone. Underneath, every joint now bends about its true axes: the bones'
orientation frames had been composed in the wrong order since the importer was written, which
folded thumbs sideways. And the posing rounds since 0.3.15: fingers and toes move alone, the face
selects the head, Ctrl + drag moves the whole figure, a dropped figure lands with a knee bend,
the Ground button is a pose edit, a kneel settles cleanly on any body.

### Added
* **Project files (`.pss`) — Save / Save As / Open the whole scene.** File → Save (`Ctrl+S`), File → Save As (`Ctrl+Shift+S`) and File → Open (`Ctrl+O`) now persist and restore a complete scene: every figure's source file, its pose (rotations, root translation, joint pins), its transform, the environment (HDRI + all lighting dials) and the camera framing. A save is tiny — kilobytes, not megabytes, because geometry and textures are never embedded; opening re-imports the referenced files and restores everything on top. A corrupt file is rejected whole with an error, so the current scene is never half-replaced. If a referenced figure file has moved, Open offers to locate it again (per missing file: Choose / Skip / Cancel) — skipped files are named in a warning and the rest of the project still loads. The scene is dirty-tracked: New, Open and closing the window ask Save / Discard Changes / Cancel when there are unsaved changes; camera moves do not count (toggleable in Preferences → General). (Developer: versioned JSON codec `ProjectDocument` in `src/viewport/scene/projectfile.{h,cpp}` — format marker + version, unknown fields ignored; figure and panorama paths are stored absolute, so a project moved to another computer asks where its files are.) (#32 — thanks @DmitryAkDev)
* **File → New (Ctrl+N) starts a fresh scene.** The File menu's `New` entry is live: it removes every model from the scene, returns the camera to home, restores the startup lighting and HDRI, resets the shader picker to PBR Shaded and the skeleton overlay off, and clears the undo history — so Ctrl+Z after New does not bring a figure back. A new scene also detaches from its saved file, so a later Save asks where to save. (#31 — thanks @DmitryAkDev)
* **The shader picker follows programmatic shade-mode changes.** The viewport's shader picker now always shows the mode the scene is actually rendered in: when the shade mode changes through any path (menu, strip, or code), the picker mirrors it — a picker that shows Matcap can no longer outlive the mode it describes. (Developer: `VulkanWindow` emits `shadeModeChanged(int)` on every change and the strip re-mirrors it; the signal deduplicates on the last announced mode so user clicks do not loop back.) (#30 — thanks @DmitryAkDev)
* **The window title names the current project.** The title bar now shows the open project's file name (never its folder) after the application name — `hero.pss — PoseStudio 0.3.x` — with an asterisk in front while the scene has unsaved changes, and no file name at all for a new, unsaved project. (Developer: document state lives in `ProjectState` (`src/viewport/projectstate.{h,cpp}`) — path + dirty flag with a deduplicating `changed()` signal; the title is assembled in `main.cpp` from that state.) (#32 — thanks @DmitryAkDev)

* **Turn a joint with the mouse: `B`, `S`, `T`.** Select a joint and press `B` to bend it, `S` to move it side to side or `T` to twist it — the three motions of the Transform tab's dials — then just move the mouse: the limb goes the way the mouse goes (for a twist, and a bend that points straight at you, right is more and left is less). A left click or `Enter` keeps the result as one undo step; `Esc`, a right click or the same key again puts the joint back. The keys work whichever panel you last clicked in (they are also under Edit → Turn Joint with Mouse), and while the mode is on the mouse is its own anywhere in the window. A badge under the viewport strip shows which motion is on. The turn stops at the joint's limits and at the body.

* **The Transform tab.** A new tab on the side panel shows the selected joint's rotation as three dials — **Bend** (the joint's natural direction), **Side-Side** (its secondary direction) and **Twist** — and lets you scrub or type them. Every dial reads on the same scale whatever the joint: 0 is the rest pose, 100 is fully bent, and negative values bend the other way in the same proportion until the joint's limit stops them (a knee reads about -7 to 100). The dials respect each joint's limits and stop at the body, the same value on a left and a right joint gives mirrored poses, the dials follow the figure when a drag, an undo or a pose file moves it, and each change is one undo step.

* **A figure dropped onto her feet absorbs the landing.** The Ground button's fall now ends the way a landing does: her knees fold, her hips dip over her planted feet and she comes back up, in under half a second — deeper the higher the drop, from a slight give for a few centimetres to a real knee bend for a metre and more. It is for the eye only: she ends in exactly the pose she was dropped in, and the dip leaves no trace. No bounce when she lands on her knees, hands or seat, or with her hips pinned.

* **Ctrl + drag the body to move the whole figure.** Ctrl + drag the hips, the belly or the chest and the figure follows the cursor exactly as she is posed — lifted off the floor, carried across the scene, set down somewhere else. Nothing is anchored to the floor while you do, no joint turns, and she neither balances nor steps; the floor still stops her on the way down. Joints you pinned stay where you pinned them, their limbs giving to keep them there. (Ctrl + drag on the chest used to bend the spine over a still pelvis, and on the hips to move the pelvis over planted feet; a limb or the head under Ctrl still moves its own chain alone.)

### Changed
* **The `X` / `Y` / `Z` + mouse wheel rotation is gone.** Holding `X`, `Y` or `Z` and rolling the wheel no longer turns the selected joint; the `B` / `S` / `T` keys and the Transform tab replace it, and they name the motion by what the joint does rather than by an axis letter. The wheel is for zooming, and for depth while you drag.
* **The Ground button is a pose edit.** Dropping a figure to the floor now changes her pose — her place — rather than moving the model under it: it is one undo step, it is saved with the pose, and Reset Pose takes it back with everything else. Before, a figure lifted in her pose, grounded and then reset stood below the floor by the height of the lift, and a pose saved after grounding loaded back in the air.
* **Fingers and toes move on their own.** Drag a finger or a toe and that digit alone bends toward the cursor, every joint of it, up to where it joins the hand or the foot — the hand, the foot and the body stay exactly where they are, with or without Ctrl. Until now a grabbed finger pulled the whole arm (and the body after it) and a grabbed toe dragged the foot. To move the hand or the foot, take hold of it rather than of a digit.
* **The face is not posed.** The bones of the face rig — the eyes, the jaw, the lips, the brows, the ears — are there for expressions: a click anywhere on the face now selects the head, the whole head lights up, and a drag from there is the head's. No drag turns a face bone.
* **Kneeling feet lie flat.** A kneeling figure's feet now lie on their tops behind her, toes pointing back — in a kneel, sitting back onto the heels, on all fours, and the rear leg of a half kneel — instead of standing on tucked toes, which was the first thing that looked wrong in every kneel. Lying face down she lies with straight legs and flat feet, and pushing up off the belly brings her onto all fours with them still flat. Getting up out of a kneel rolls the feet back onto their toes first, the knees lifting as the toes tuck under; a knee drawn up out of a flat kneel swings its foot forward and lands it flat.
* **Standing on one leg puts the weight over the standing foot.** Raise a knee, kick, put a foot up on a step or lift one behind, and the hips now move over the foot she stands on as the other leaves the floor — 22cm on the base figure's wide stance — the standing leg angling in under her; brought back down, she stands as she stood. Until now the hips stayed put and she stood on one leg with her weight a hand's width beside the standing foot, a stance that would topple, in every such pose. Balance polices what the shift cannot know (where the lifted leg's own mass goes), so the weight ends inside the standing footprint on every reference figure. A foot already in the air when a drag begins shifts nothing.
* **A foot set down from a tilted carry rolls flat over its descent** instead of snapping flat in two ticks (a foot carried far sideways on a straight leg rides the shin, tilted 50 degrees; the snap moved a toe 85mm in one tick on the newest figure generation).
* **Standing on one leg has a hip.** Raise a knee, kick or lift a foot behind and the pelvis now drops a few degrees on the free side, the standing hip hiking, while the chest stays level and the spine curves gently between them — the relaxed one-leg stance rather than a level pelvis over a trunk stiff as a post. Bring the foot down and she stands level again.
* **A kneeling figure keeps her knees down when her chest is pulled past her reach.** Pulled forward and down beyond what her trunk can give, a kneeler used to rise onto her hands and toes in a plank (a toon character with a short trunk did, from an ordinary all-fours drag, and popped a finger 42cm when lifted back up); the knees now stay on the floor and the body goes on down onto its hands, as a person's would. Only a pull UP beyond her reach frees the knees, which is what gets her up.
* **Clicking selects what is under the cursor.** A click on the figure now picks the surface it lands on and the joint that moves that skin, so a part hidden behind another can no longer be selected through it: clicking the chest gets the chest, not the shoulder or the arm behind it. A click right on a joint still grabs that joint.
* **Ctrl + drag poses one part at a time.** Ctrl + drag a limb or the head and only the chain it belongs to follows the cursor: a hand or foot moves its limb up to the body, the head bends the neck. Nothing else moves — no balance, no step, no automatic arm or head motion. (On the body itself Ctrl + drag moves the whole figure: see Added.) Turning a single joint is the job of the new `B` / `S` / `T` keys and the Transform tab (see Added).
* **Getting up off the belly no longer snaps an arm.** From lying face down, dragging the hips up brought her onto all fours with a jolt: an arm folded under the chest let go in a single frame partway up. The hands laid on the floor now turn onto their palms only as fast as the arm can follow, so the arms straighten smoothly as the body rises. On heavier builds whose prone does not lie flat, the same drag used to fling a hand and stand the figure up on hands and feet; now the knees stay down and she comes onto all fours, the lifted hand hanging by her side.
* **A hand laid on the body lies on its palm.** Drag a hand to the hip, the chest, the top of the head, the belly or a knee and it now turns to lie flat there — palm to the surface, fingers along it — the way a hand put on the floor already did; brought to the small of the back it rests on the back of the hand, because the forearm cannot turn the palm round that far. Before, the hand kept whatever angle the forearm gave it, standing edge-on beside a hip or lying across the face palm down, and every hand-on-hip pose needed the wrist turned by hand afterwards. A hand merely dragged past the body is left alone: the hand settles onto what it rests against, not what it passes.
* **The collarbone moves like a collarbone.** A hand dragged far — straight up beyond the arm's reach, or behind the head in one motion — used to hoist the shoulder up onto the neck: the collar bone swung and twisted to its limits and rose 55 degrees to give the reach the arm did not have. Under a hand drag it now rises 30 degrees at most and swings or twists 15, what a clavicle does; a high reach shrugs like a shoulder, and what the arm cannot reach it no longer fakes.
* **Reaching high goes on tiptoe.** Pull a hand up past the arm's reach and she rises onto the balls of her feet — heels up, body tall — before a further pull lifts her off the floor; bring the hand back within reach and the heels come down. Before, the feet stayed flat and the hand stopped a hand's breadth short of the cursor until she was lifted.
* **Idle hands relax.** The fingers of an arm that hangs curl gently into a relaxed hand while you pose (each joint a quarter of the way into its range, the thumb turned in a little) instead of standing flat and spread as the figure imports, and a hand coming down onto the floor opens again onto its palm. Fingers you have posed yourself are left alone; Reset Pose flattens them.
* **Reaching down folds at the hips.** Drag a hand down and the trunk now folds forward at the hips as it goes — a bow over softened knees for a hand brought to the knee, a fold at the hips and the knees for a hand taken to the floor, the toe-touch's fold on some figures — the hips going back to keep the balance and the head looking down where the hand goes. Until now a hand pushed down was answered by a squat with the trunk bolt upright and the gaze level, the hands between the knees for a reach to the floor. Brought back up, she stands as she stood. Standing starts only: a seated figure still reaches from its seat.

### Fixed
* **Each joint of a finger or a toe lights up on its own.** Clicking the first or the second joint of a finger lit the same two segments either way, so there was no telling which joint was selected; a toe's two joints had the same problem. The second joint — a hinge — was being taken for the first joint's twist bone, which is lit together with its limb. Every finger and toe joint now lights its own segment.
* **Joints bend about their true axes.** Each bone's orientation frame was composed from the figure file's three angles in the wrong order. Where one angle dominates — a thigh, a shin, an upper arm — the frame was within two degrees of right and nobody could tell; where two or three are large it was not: a forearm bent about an axis 11 degrees off its own, a foot 7, a finger 8, and a thumb 44 to 56 — a thumb curled one joint at a time (the old X/Y/Z wheel, the new Transform tab) folded sideways across the palm instead of toward it. Every joint of every figure generation now turns about the axes the figure authors; thumbs and fingers curl as they should, by the wheel, the dials and a drag alike. **Poses saved with earlier versions load slightly differently** at those joints: the numbers in the file are the same, the axes they turn about are the right ones now.
* **Getting up out of a kneel lifts the feet to turn them over.** A kneeling figure's feet lie on their tops, toes pointing back; to stand she needs them on their pads, toes forward. They now come a little off the floor as the knees rise, turn over in the air and land on their toes, the way a person's do. Three small faults in the same hand-over were fixed with it (a foot target that ran 3cm ahead of the foot, a leftover pull toward the lying position, and the standing foot's heading read off the wrong axis — on most figures she now stands up with her heels flat, where they used to end 6 to 8mm off the floor).
* **A finger pulled far beyond its reach no longer flips while you hold it still.** Pulled along its own length a finger folds into a zigzag, and on one figure generation it stayed in it until a third of a second after the cursor stopped, then swung its middle joint straight in a single frame. It now leaves the zigzag while you are still dragging.
* **A limb brought against the body no longer makes the pose jump.** The solver accepted a step by a cost that knew only the collision surfaces within reach *before* the step, so a fast-moving limb could swing a centimetre and more into the chest unseen, be thrown out again by the next iteration, and leave the pose wherever the iterations ran out. Lying down from all fours threw the head 26cm in one frame on one custom body and popped 10–15cm on four others; it is smooth on all 23 test bodies now (1–7cm at the fastest joint), a figure reclining on the floor holds still where her hands used to twitch 4cm, and a knee drawn up out of a kneel on the oldest figure generation jumps half as far. Every step is now judged by the surfaces it actually meets, and a limb's own collision surface fades in from its ends instead of switching on a finger's width from the joint.
* **A kneel settles without a twitch.** As a kneeling figure's feet rolled onto their tops, the ankle was asked back to the spot the foot had stood on, which a shin lying on the floor cannot reach; the leftover pull sat on the leg through the whole pose, and on two long-footed custom bodies both shins snapped from one side to the other a moment after the kneel was made (3–4cm at a toe). The lying ankle is now asked to where its shin actually lies from the planted knee, and the kneel comes to rest (about 1cm at most on every test body).
* **A heavy figure pulled up out of a kneel steps under herself.** On a body with thick feet the ankle of a foot lying flat rests a little above its standing height, which the rig took for a figure still hovering over the floor, and it held back every step for the rest of the drag: pulled up by the chest she ended on tiptoe, short of the cursor. A foot with its toes on the floor is not hovering; she now takes the two small steps her base figure takes and stands.
* **Lying down from all fours lets the hands go gradually.** The hold that keeps a hand on the floor while the body pushes up was released during a descent by weakening it, which let the hand go all at once at the end (13cm at a fingertip on one custom body). It is now released by raising it, so the wrist comes up at a steady pace.
* **The two oldest figure generations' thighs could not move inward past straight under IK.** The solver's rule for hinge joints (a knee, an elbow: a wide range one side of straight, a few degrees the other) matched their thigh's sideways channel, whose authored limits are −85..15 degrees, so a standing thigh asked to bring the pelvis over its foot twisted 12 degrees instead and the foot pivoted a centimetre. A hinge must now also have a narrow second channel, which a thigh does not.

## [0.3.15] - 2026-09-24

The posing release. Full-body IK runs on a new joint-space solver — no trembling, no lag, the grabbed
point on the cursor — and the body now crouches, kneels, sits, lies down, gets onto all fours and
onto its belly and up again, lunges, half-kneels, sways, hinges, bows and walks, with its arms
hanging and its head up, on every figure generation and on custom characters of any size. Joint
pins make seats. Figures import standing on the floor. A user manual lives under Help. The posing
engine is verified by an in-repo harness of 122 phases on eight reference rigs, a 23-character
sweep, and a scripted in-app suite of 152 expectations that renders and compares every pose.

### Added
* **A User Manual, inside the app.** Help → User Manual (or `F1`) opens a manual of everything that works in this build — getting started, the interface, importing, navigating, the shading modes, lighting, posing with a page of step-by-step poses, pose files, the Asset Manager, Preferences, a keyboard-and-mouse reference, privacy, troubleshooting — with a table of contents, search across every page, and back/forward. Help → Release Notes opens its What's New page, which is this changelog. The manual is written as plain Markdown in `docs/manual/` and embedded at build time, so it always matches the version you run; a checker (`tools/manual/check_manual.py`, also a CTest test) keeps its links whole and warns when a menu entry goes undocumented. (Developer: `src/help/`, `docs/manual/README.md` for how to update it.)
* **A character sweep for the posing test harness.** The harness's figure gates are calibrated on eight base figures; a custom character differs in exactly the things those numbers were tuned on. `tools/ikharness/sweep/` dumps the rig of every figure preset you list (through the real application, so it is the rig the app would pose), runs the whole harness on each, and sorts the failed gates into near-misses — a calibrated number a few percent over — and real failures. Its first run over 23 custom characters found four posing bugs that no base figure shows (all fixed below). Every phase also reports how LOPSIDED the pose came out, and a symmetric gesture — the hips or the chest dragged straight down, back or forward from a symmetric pose — must now stay symmetric: legs folded over to one side are invisible from the side view such a drag is made in. (The gate is applied on figures that are themselves symmetric at rest, which every base figure is; a character's own small asymmetries are amplified, rightly - a leg a millimetre longer stands a few degrees more bent at the knee - so there the result is reported, not failed.)
* **The in-app posing galleries run on custom characters.** The scripts name bones and drag in screen pixels, so they run on any figure: three galleries on six very different bodies (a toon, a teenager, a dwarf, a heavy build, a man, the character from a user's report) found what no harness number could (fixed below) - and one fault in the test tool itself: a `press` is the real picker at the bone's pixel, and on another body that pixel can belong to another limb (a broad character's hanging arm covers her chest from the side; one hand covers the other), so a gallery of "chest" drags had been hauling an upper arm about. When the pick lands on another part of the body than the script names, the bone is now grabbed by name at the same point and the log says so; on the base figure a "right hand" press turned out to have been posing the LEFT hand all along.
* **The in-app posing tests see skin and pops.** A floor-poses script (toward all fours, sat back on the heels, reclined from a sit, a leg stretched out) with a new measure, `meshlow` — the lowest point of the posed SKIN over the floor, which no joint reading shows — and the runner now logs every single-frame jump over 10cm with the joint that made it. That is how this round's floor problems were found.
* **The in-app posing tests cover the views and the figures people actually use.** Two more scripts — gestures from the Home and three-quarter perspective views, and whole poses built from several drags in a row (a walk, a sit, a high reach, picking something up) — and the scripts now run on every figure generation: bones are named as the reference figure names them and mapped onto the others. Running the side gallery on the newest generation is what showed its head thrown back under a head pull, which no numeric gate had measured; the harness now reports where the head looks on every phase. (Developer: `figureBoneName` in `vulkanwindow_script.cpp`; the `orbit` command takes degrees.)
* **The in-app posing scripts are a test suite.** `expect <metric> <op> <value>` lines assert on what the running application did — the grabbed point on the cursor (in millimetres or on screen), steps, the head's gaze, a joint's height or travel, a posed channel, nothing through the floor — the application exits with the number that failed, and `tools/ikscripts/run_all.sh` runs every script (three galleries and a numbers-only returns check, 78 expectations), prints a pass/fail summary and compares each rendered shot with a reference: the poses that LOOK different are laid out beside their references in one image per script. The references are renders of a figure preset and stay outside the repository. (Developer: `VulkanWindow::scriptMetric`, `tools/ikscripts/compare.py`.)
* **A scripted in-app posing test for developers.** `POSESTUDIO_IK_SCRIPT=<file>` runs a script of real picks, presses, screen-space drags, releases, pins and view changes through the running application — no mouse or keyboard input is injected, so it is safe on a desktop in use — prints what a user would see as numbers, and saves the rendered frames as PNGs read back from the GPU (the window need not be visible). `tools/ikscripts/` has two gesture galleries, a runner that builds a contact sheet of the shots, and the command reference. It exists so that posing changes are looked at, not only measured: its first run threw out a change that every numeric gate had passed (a "lean back instead of stepping back" rule that left her tilted like a plank with her weight behind her heels). (Developer: `vulkanwindow_script.cpp`, `ikscript.h`; `VulkanRenderer::requestCapture` / `takeCapture`, swapchain images created with `TRANSFER_SRC` where the surface allows.)

### Changed
* **The posing solve is six files instead of one function.** `Armature::solveIk` had grown to one 3,800-line function; it is now an orchestrator of seventeen stage functions over one shared per-tick state (`armatureiksolvestate.h`, every member documented), spread over `armatureiksolvedrag.cpp` (what the drag is), `armatureiksolveposture.cpp` (the stiffness model), `armatureiksolverows.cpp` (the rows the solver satisfies) and `armatureikpost.cpp` (the arms' hang and the head's righting), with every tuning constant and its reasoning in `armatureiksolvetuning.h`. The split was mechanical and is verified bit-identical on all eight figure generations (122 harness phases each), so nothing a user sees changed. Four rejected variants that had stayed in the code behind environment probes are gone, a duplicated knee test is one rig helper, and the drag-state trace is readable. Two determinism phases guard round 89's exact-bind fix: a pose brought back to zero IS the bind, and a drag repeated after Reset Pose ends in the same pose to the last bit. A structural audit of every posing-side file followed: dead accessors and a dead step trigger removed, duplicated helpers merged (the bone-alias table the harness and the in-app scripts each carried their own drifting copy of is one file, `scene/ik/bonealiases.h`), a latent Windows build break (`far` as a variable name) removed, index guards added, and the harness made safe against a phase whose bone a rig lacks.
* **Imported figures stand on the floor.** A figure is now placed with the lowest point of its skin on the floor when it is imported. Characters are authored a little off it - their shape moves their soles: across 23 custom characters, from 6cm below the floor to 5cm above it, and even the base figures rest 5-8mm up - and everything that reasons about the floor (the posing, the Ground button, the ground shadow) starts from a figure that is on it.
* **Posing runs on a new whole-body solver: no trembling and no lag.** Dragging a joint used to trade two things against each other — the damping that kept the body from trembling was also what made it lag behind the cursor and take a second to catch up. The solver underneath has been replaced by one that does not make that trade: every tick it solves the whole pose directly in the figure's own joint angles, inside their authored limits, to convergence. The grabbed joint sits on the cursor (0.01mm at rest, 3mm behind at a moderate drag, 6mm during a fast flick — and that small trail is a deliberate softening of its starts and stops, not catch-up); a still cursor gives a perfectly still figure with nothing frozen; bringing the cursor back brings the body back exactly where it was; planted feet and pinned joints hold to microns; the body's follow-through eases in over a few frames rather than snapping; and pushing the chest or a hand down crouches the figure as far as you push. Everything the old solver did carries over — planted feet and ground healing, balance, stepping and walking the figure by its hips, lifting it off the floor and landing it, hands and knees planting on the floor, crouching, kneeling, sitting, joint pins, self-collision — and several of those are better for it (see Fixed). The old solver, and the layers of damping and after-the-fact correction it needed, are gone. (Developer: `scene/ik/jointsolver.{h,cpp}` — regularized nonlinear least squares, Levenberg-Marquardt with an analytic Jacobian in the dual form, limits as an active-set box, one-sided rows for the floor and the body volumes, fold channels with a straight-limb retry; `scene/armatureiksolve.cpp` — the tasks, the range-normalized stiffness model, the ball-of-foot contact model, the posture easing and the target follower; `IkRig` keeps the drag's policy — `ikrigpolicy.cpp` replaces `ikrigsolve.cpp`. Removed: `fabriksolver`, `ikconstraints`, `armatureikextract.cpp`, `armatureikpins.cpp`, `armatureik_detail.h`, the governors, the settle-freeze, the rotational prior, the twist witness, the pin refinement, the pelvis-tilt assist, the heel-lift policy. Harness: the `[unit] joint-space solver` phase; all eight figure generations pass 114/114, with the gates that encoded the old solver's behaviour re-set and annotated in `realphases.cpp`. See CLAUDE.md's FBIK section.)

### Fixed
* **A pinned pelvis kept its meaning through a lift.** The rig remembered a seated figure's pelvis pin by its position in the pin list, and never renumbered it when that list shrank — after a lift released the contact pins, "the seat" named another pin or none, and the seated posture rules (no balance, no steps, the pelvis rocking under a chest drag) switched off or attached themselves to a hand. Found by the audit; the index now follows the pin.
* **Posing intents scale with the figure.** Four of the rig's intent gates — a pelvis drag counted as going DOWN from 2cm below its start, a limb pushing the body down from 15cm below itself, pulling it up from 10cm above, a crouch from 8cm — were fixed centimetres where every other geometric threshold of the rig scales with the figure's size; on a half-size character they were twice as coarse. They scale now (the full-size figures are unchanged; on the 23-character sweep the smallest body's prone pose sinks 1mm into the floor where it sank 9).
* **Posing: Reset Pose is a clean slate, and a repeated drag repeats exactly.** A joint brought back to exactly zero (Reset Pose, Reset Joint) was left a hair off its rest position by floating-point rounding, so the same drag made twice from the "same" pose could end differently — visibly, on a touchy gesture like pushing up off the belly, which came up on straight arms after a fresh load and on folded ones after a reset. A zero rotation now composes to the rest pose exactly. (Developer: `Armature::recomposePoseLocal`; `IK_DRAG_STATE_TRACE` prints a pose checksum at every press and tick.)
* **Posing: lying on her belly works on more bodies.** A heavy character and the newest figure generation used to stop on straight arms with the hips in the air — or worse, rear up on the arms until the hands let go and the body dropped 30-40cm in one frame. A body being taken down by the hips no longer lets go of the hands and knees it is coming down onto, so she lies down on them: the newest generation lies flat now, and the heavy character too. (The third figure generation still stands on its arms — its wrists run out of range.) (Developer: `IkRig::updateContacts`, no lift-off under a descent; `IK_LIFT_OFF_DESCENDING`.)
* **Posing: a hand dragged along the floor lies on its palm.** Pull a hand out from under her when she lies on her belly and it used to go forward any way the arm found cheapest — as often with the elbow swung out sideways and the wrist twisted to its limit as with the arm extending. The hand now lies flat, fingers along the way it is dragged, and the arm unfolds as it goes; the push-up that follows (hands under the shoulders, then the hips up) comes up on straight arms. (Developer: the hand slide in `armatureiksolve.cpp`; `IK_JS_NO_HAND_SLIDE`.)
* **Posing: on the third figure generation the toes no longer go through the floor in a kneel.** That generation hangs every toe straight off the mid-foot, and the posing had been treating one toe as the ball of the foot and letting the other four ride rigidly — in a kneel they pointed straight down with the foot, 26mm into the floor, on every kneel, sit-back and all-fours of that generation. All five toes now bend back together as the foot pitches over them; the skin rests on the floor. Other generations are unchanged. (Developer: `m_jsToeMates` in `armatureiksolve.cpp`; probes `IK_JS_NO_TOE_MATES`, `IK_JS_BALL_TRACE`.)
* **Posing: lying on her belly works again, and she gets up off it.** Dragging the hips forward and down from all fours had stopped a hand's breadth short — she stayed on all fours with her head tucked under, on every figure — since the change that keeps a hand on the floor while she pushes up off her belly: that same rule was holding her hands down while she was trying to come DOWN onto her arms. It now applies only while the body is going up. Two contact rules came with it: a hand under an elbow resting on the floor is a support in its own right (it was being ignored in favour of the elbow, so a push-up off the belly lost the hands in the first centimetres and she came up with both hands in the air), and an elbow beside a planted hand is not one (it dragged along the floor and let go with a jolt as the arm straightened). She lies flat on six of the eight figure generations and on four of five custom characters tried; the gallery's push-up off the belly passes as written. The harness now GATES the prone descent where it can (it had been informational everywhere, which is how the break went unseen). (Developer: `m_jsTargetDown`, `IkRig::servedByPinAbove`/`servedByHandBelow`, `RunResult::liveLiftOffs`; probes `IK_JS_HAND_CEILING_ALWAYS`, `IK_ELBOW_SERVES_HAND`, `IK_ELBOW_CONTACTS`, `IK_JS_CEILING_TRACE`.)
* **Posing: the half kneel lands without a pop, and the kneeling knee stays put.** Drawing a knee up out of a kneel used to snap the foot's landing spot into place and the leg after it (a toe jumping 6-10cm in one frame on the base figures, 10cm on two custom characters), roll the lifted thigh 20 degrees in a frame on the male figure, and — on four custom characters — slide the OTHER knee, the one she is kneeling on, 4-7cm across the floor. The foot's landing target now moves at the same eased pace as every other target; a raised knee is asked no further out to the side than the hip can take it at that flexion (a thigh flexed to horizontal has no abduction left — that channel only rolls it, and rolling it was the pose's cheapest way out); the leg's twist is priced by what the channel actually does as it stands, not by its name; the kneeling knee is a hard support while the other leg is drawn; and a foot letting go of the floor is forgiven the depth its tucked toes already stood "through" it by (one figure generation's toe tips read 15-18mm under, and the floor pushing them out threw the knee 8cm sideways). Toe pops halve on every figure generation (25-42mm), the male's roll is gone, and the four characters' kneeling knees move under a hundredth of a millimetre. (Developer: `kneeHangUnder`, the lateral reach clamp and the twist-by-axis price in `armatureiksolve.cpp`; the hard knee in `IkRig::boundLivePins`; `floorAllow`; probes `IK_JS_KNEE_TRACE`, `IK_HARNESS_EULER_TICKS`, `IK_JS_CONTACT_FADE_TRACE`, `IK_BOUND_TRACE`.)
* **Posing: a bow keeps its feet on every body, and undoes cleanly.** A top-heavy character bowed by the chest used to shuffle two steps forward (the bow's forward travel was being treated as a walk's); the follow now stands down for a fold, the weight rests a little less past the toes, and a bow let go of and then taken back up stands her upright — the hips come back under the trunk as it rises (she used to end leaning 5-10 degrees at the hips).
* **Posing: lifted off all fours by the chest, she kneels up taller.** The hips now come up to the kneeling height as the trunk comes upright (they used to stay a few centimetres low, and the chest with them): the chest rests 10-24mm from the cursor on every figure generation where it rested 20-34, with the pelvis upright, and two heavy custom characters that could not kneel up cleanly now do.
* **Posing: the half kneel on any body, and knee draws that follow the cursor.** A knee drawn up out of a kneel used to be hauled 10-23cm above where the cursor took it (the foot's hang asking the ankle closer to the knee than the shin is long, which folded the knee and, on a short-legged character, flung the foot up over it); the knee now rises exactly as far as it is dragged, the foot slides forward along the floor under it and swings under once the knee is a shin's length up, and a half kneel lands the foot flat under the knee — on every figure generation and on the characters that could not make one. Every knee draw is calmer (the knee's worst tick 18-57mm where it was 60-71).
* **Solver: a channel that runs out of range mid-step no longer stalls the solve.** A joint the model was counting on and its limit cut short broke every step (seven rejected dampings a tick); the step is now scaled to the limit and the joint blocked, the standard active-set ratio test.
* **Posing: a kneeling knee no longer twitches when first drawn up.** On three older figure generations a knee drawn up out of a kneel shot 10cm out to the side and back in the drag's first centimetres; it now rises cleanly on every generation (the foot's sole was being turned flat while the foot still stood on its tucked toes).
* **Posing: dragged across the floor by the chest, she steps sooner.** A foot a stride from where the stance wants it steps at once instead of after a fifth of a second's confirmation — a fast drag's first step now begins during the drag rather than as the cursor stops.
* **Posing: the half kneel.** Kneel her, then draw one knee up to hip height and forward: the foot below it swings under the rising knee and lands ahead, flat, the other knee staying down. It used to trail behind at knee height with the shin folded up against the thigh, and the thigh twisted 60 degrees (a frog's leg).
* **Posing: lifted off all fours by the chest, she comes up onto her knees.** Drag the chest up and back over the hips from all fours and she kneels up - knees on the floor, trunk up over them, arms hanging, wrists relaxed; from the kneel a second drag stands her. She used to rise straight toward her feet with her trunk still folded and her knees in the air, and on three older figure generations her planted hands held her trunk down (their hands stand on their fingertips, or their collars run sideways from the chest, and the rule that keeps a hand planted while its arm has length to give never let go). A pair of hands that lift together no longer lift lopsided (one hung, one stayed nailed to the floor).
* **Posing: her arms hang as she gets up off all fours.** Lifted off all fours by the chest she used to come up with both arms held straight out in front of her: the idle-arm hang wanted them down, but its own collision stop refused every step — on all fours the shoulders sit shrugged forward, the upper arm rests against the chest, and swinging it down pressed it a hair deeper into a chest that is wider lower down. A hanging arm resting against the body now hangs a little further out, where the body lets it, and slides down along it. Fixed with it: a hand on the floor now hangs from its straight arm as the body rises - it stays down under a push-up and lifts off cleanly under a chest that keeps going (one hand used to stay a contact and its wrist flipped 27cm in a tick).
* **Posing: she gets up off her belly.** Lying face down, drag the hips up and she comes up onto her hands and knees (drag each hand forward and down onto the floor ahead of her first for a proper push-up position); from there the hips or the chest stand her up. Her hands used to leave the floor with her trunk as the hips rose - she came up into a kneeling plank with both hands in the air - and her knees let go and swung under her: a hand whose arm still has length to give now stays on the floor while the body rises (a push-up), and a knee the hips were low over when the drag began stays a support for the whole drag. A toon character's twitch in the prone hold (23cm in one tick) is gone with it.
* **Posing: she can lie on her belly.** From all fours, drag the hips far forward and down and she lies face down, thighs forward from her knees, shins folded up behind her, hands where they were. Three things stood in the way: her head, touching the floor ahead of her, was being planted as a support (she stood on it, a tripod of knees and face); her pelvis was priced to stay upright on planted knees, so her thigh hit its limit with her hips 30cm up; and her feet were held to the floor behind planted knees. Pushing the chest down from all fours also lowers her now instead of raising her hips into a head-stand. (By the chest alone it is not yet a full prone - her planted hands stay put and her arms fold under her.)
* **Posing: crossed legs no longer overlap at the knee on fuller figures.** A knee is as wide as the thigh it ends, but the self-collision model kept only a bare joint's clearance for it once two thighs touched at the knees rather than along their length — on three characters with fuller thighs the crossed legs ended with the knees a centimetre and a half inside each other. The knee (and the elbow against the torso) now keeps its segment's flesh.
* **Posing: she can be put on her hands and knees.** Kneel her (drag the hips down and forward), then drag the chest forward and down until her hands touch the floor: she folds at the hips over her knees, her arms hang straight down under the tilting trunk, and each hand rolls onto its flat palm as it lands, fingers forward. Before this there was no way onto all fours — from a kneel the chest stopped a foot short of the cursor with the spine curled to its limit and the hands in the air, and hands pushed to the floor were answered by a squat. Fixed on the way there: her knees no longer lift off the floor as the chest goes forward (balance was pulling the hips up onto her toes), the chest no longer runs ahead of the cursor once the hands are down, and a figure taken down onto all fours and back up again in one drag lets go of the floor with her hands instead of standing in a forward fold. In deep bows her arms now hang plumb rather than trailing behind her.
* **Posing: grabbing a figure by the chest - or the face - follows the cursor.** A click on a figure's chest lands on a small chest-muscle bone as often as on the chest itself (always, on a fuller bust), and a click on the face lands on an eye or a brow bone. Those grabs drag the chest or the head, but the grabbed point was tied to it by an offset that did not turn with the body: pull the chest sideways and the point under the cursor slid 4-5cm away as she leaned, trailing the cursor by 3-4cm all the way. The offset is read off the pose every frame now: a chest grab stays within 1-2cm on a far sideways pull (what remains is the lean itself: a point beside the spine drops as the chest rolls), and an eye grab rests 1mm from the cursor instead of 10.
* **Posing: on the newest figure generation a kneeling figure sits back onto her heels upright.** She reclined 40 degrees with her head tipped up instead of folding at the hips. That generation's thigh bones carry their twist helpers as extra children, which made the posing take them for the pelvis - and on planted knees the pelvis's tilt is deliberately expensive, so her hips would not fold. The pelvis is now recognised as the bone both legs hang from, on every rig. Found by looking at a custom character of that generation in the floor gallery; no number had measured a trunk's lean on the heels (one does now).
* **Posing: symmetric gestures stay symmetric.** Two lopsided results the new symmetry measure found on the base figures: getting down onto all fours landed one hand 4cm ahead of the other on six of the eight reference figures (the idle arms are turned one after the other, and each was being stopped against the other where that loop had just left it - the stop is order-independent now), and a sit pushed well past the floor ended with both knees 5-7cm over to one side on two generations (the floor contact points picked on the pelvis's skin were not a mirror-symmetric set - they are now).
* **Posing works on custom characters, not only on the base figures.** A morphed character could not kneel - her knees stopped in the air and her hips tipped into an unnatural angle looking for the missing depth - because the posing reads the floor off the figure's own rest pose, and a character whose shape lifts her soles a few centimetres off the floor no longer had recognisable balls of the feet: her ankles were held rigid, with no heel lift and no tucked toes. Figures are put on the floor at import now (above), and she kneels with her knees on the floor and her torso upright, and sits. Three more faults that only showed on unusual bodies are fixed with it: **a crouch that locked solid** a few centimetres down on some characters (a joint a hair inside its limit made the solver reject every step - the same trap that used to stop a figure short when she was sat back up from lying); **a short character brought down onto hands and knees could not be taken back up in the same drag** (the knees, planted hard on the way down, were never let go while the hips stayed low - now they are as soon as the hips come back up 3cm); and **a pinned hand was ignored under an elbow drag on short-armed characters** (the rule asked for 20cm of forearm; it is a share of the limb now, and the pin holds to a thousandth of a millimetre).
* **Mirror Pose mirrors every bone of the main figure family.** That family's base file spells about thirty left/right bone ids differently on its two sides (a big toe's second joint, four bones of the palm, the lip corners, the eyelids), so they found no partner by name and were flipped in place like a bone on the body's centre line: a mirrored hand kept its own palm bones' pose. Bones without a name partner are now paired by where they stand - and a name partner is only accepted if it stands at the mirrored place: the same file calls one bone of the palm `…Carpal2` on the left and a different one on the right.
* **Posing: kneeling no longer jumps.** Dragging the hips down and forward into a kneel could throw them 20cm down and back in the single frame the knees touched the floor — and leave them there, well away from the cursor. Each knee was being nailed to the spot it hovered over just before landing and forced straight down onto the floor there, which a tucked-under foot often cannot follow. Now a knee comes down where the leg can put it and is planted where it lands, the toes behind a planted knee may shift a little, and pushing the hips lower than kneeling legs allow no longer tips the pelvis forward 50° under an arched back — she stops where her thighs stop her (drag the hips back to sit onto the heels, which no longer pops either). (Developer: `kKneeLandingHold`, `kKneelFootHold`, `kKneelTiltScale` in `armatureiksolve.cpp`, `IkRig::reseatLivePin`; harness phase "kneel, a little deeper and shorter than the rig's own".)
* **Posing: you can lay her down.** A figure sitting on the floor, leaned back by the chest, used to arch her spine as far as it went over hips that stayed pointed up, and stop with her chest a forearm's length above the floor. Now her pelvis rolls back with her trunk: drag the chest down behind her and she lies down on her back, spine straight, knees up, her arms coming to rest beside her — and drag the chest back up and she sits up again, arms hanging by her sides. While you move a seated figure by her chest or head her hands no longer plant themselves on the floor as props (they folded the arms one way and the other as she went down); pin a hand (P) if it should stay. (Developer: for a drag that begins with the pelvis resting on the floor the root's pitch and height REFERENCES follow the target about the seat — `seatRollDeg` / `seatRollLift` in `Armature::solveIk`; a lying figure is sat up, not stood up, by one drag; idle arms are carried clear of the floor by `hangIdleArms`; `IkRig::m_seatedTrunkDrag`. Probe `IK_JS_NO_SEAT_ROLL`.)
* **Posing: a cursor held far out of reach pulls as hard as it should.** The solver's internal model bounded how much of a far target it asked for per step — and, by an oversight, weakened the pull by the same factor: a target 30cm out of reach pulled a sixth as hard in the model as in the cost it was minimizing. The pose froze somewhere between the two answers (every step rejected, which is also why holding a joint out of reach was the most expensive thing the solver did), and "she gets stuck" was partly this. The bound now keeps the true pull. Two old bugs it had been hiding were fixed with it: on the oldest figure generation the "flat foot" target of a tucked-toe kneel pointed backwards (she stood up on 3cm of heel), and on the newest the idle-arm hang could twist a shoulder 78° about itself under a deep fold. (Developer: `JointSolver::residuals` / `linearize` — r = sqrt(w/k)·k·e, J = sqrt(w/k)·J_geo; probe `IK_JS_OLD_ASK`; new diagnostics `IK_JS_ROW_TRACE`, `IK_JS_GRAD_TRACE`, `IK_JS_FAR_ROWS`, `IK_JS_JACOBIAN_CHECK`.)
* **Posing: steadier on the floor.** The floor now stops the body's lowest points of skin themselves, not a once-per-frame estimate of them, so a hand resting on the floor under a moving arm no longer chatters; a floor contact's grip eases on and off instead of switching (a held sit could make a planted hand flicker); and a pelvis rolling over its own seat keeps its grip on the floor. Sitting back onto the heels from a kneel now ends upright instead of thrown back. (Developer: floor rows on `IkRig::fleshPoints`, `IkRig::liveContactRise`, `m_jsContactGone`.)
* **Posing: the floor stops her body, not just her joints.** Sit her down, kneel her, lay her back, and her flesh used to sink into the floor — up to 8cm of it — because everything above the feet was stopped only when the JOINT inside it came within 1.5cm of the floor. The floor now stops her skin: each part of the body knows how far its own flesh reaches below it as it is turned (20cm under the hips when she sits, 13 behind the chest when she lies back, 6 in front of a kneeling knee), so you can drag the hips down until they stop and she is sitting on the floor, not in it. And a figure sitting on the floor is now held up by her seat: before, she was "standing on her feet" as far as balance knew, 40cm off them — lean her back by the chest and her arms were flung out as counterweights the moment her hands touched the floor (70cm in one frame). Now she leans back onto her hands. (Developer: `IkRig::fitFloorClearances` — the support function of each bone's own skin, from the body mesh sample, read along "down" in the bone's frame every tick; a pelvis resting on the floor is a live contact under any drag but its own, and balance stands down while it does, `IkRig::floorSeat`. Probes `IK_FLOOR_NO_MESH`, `IK_NO_FLOOR_SEAT`.)
* **Posing: no more pops when a hanging hand lands or a kneeling figure gets up.** An arm the app had been letting hang was snapped back toward its old pose the moment the solver took it over, and a kneeling figure's knee held the floor until the last moment and then swung 16cm in one frame on some characters. Both hand over smoothly now. (Developer: `m_jsPostStepEuler`; the live contact's hold fades with `m_jsRootRise`.)
* **Posing: dragging the head moves her body instead of breaking her neck.** Pull the head back and one figure generation threw its head back to the sky while another looked at the floor; pull it 15cm to the side and the neck bent to its 40° limit under a body that barely moved; bow her by the head and she tucked her chin and stared at her own feet. * **The Ground button lets the figure fall.** Instead of jumping to the floor, a hovering figure now drops in free fall, 9.81 m/s² from rest on real time, so a metre of height lands in about half a second, and stops exactly on the ground. A figure sunk below the floor is still lifted out in one step.
* **The grabbed joint now sticks to the cursor.** The joint you drag is closed onto the cursor exactly every tick through its own limb — the same joint-space fit that holds pinned joints — while the rest of the body keeps its damped, balanced follow-through underneath. Hand lag at a moderate drag speed is halved (about 1cm, from 2cm), a fast flick's lag drops four-fold, the hand no longer rests short of the cursor when it stops, and idle-joint trembling during a slow pull is gone entirely. The cursor filter opens fully while moving, since the fit no longer amplifies pointer noise.
* **IK drags reach where you point — the elbow's fold plane now turns.** The solver carried no twist at all, so an arm could only fold in the plane its drag-start pose left it in: a hand pulled in front of the chest stopped up to 10cm short of a plainly reachable point and sat there, which read as lag. Elbows now bend in any plane the upper arm's authored twist range allows, the pose extraction realizes that twist exactly, a finger grab keeps tracking the finger as the hand turns, and the grabbed joint's own motion is speed-shaped. Measured on the real figure: the resting miss after a fast flick 103mm → 33mm, an overhead reach 11cm higher, and idle-joint trembling during slow pulls down 4-18×.
* **Pulling the head up straightens the back.** A head or chest pulled upward used to move only the neck, because the rule that keeps an arm raise from bending the spine excluded the whole trunk. Trunk gestures now keep the spine in the solve: a slouched figure straightens as its head is lifted.
* **Crouches keep both feet on the floor.** Pushing the chest down or dragging the hip into a squat used to bury one foot up to the ankle, because a knee could only fold in the plane it started in while the pelvis dropped and rolled. Knees now turn their fold plane through the thigh's authored twist range during crouches, walks, and foot drags, so the feet stay planted; the child-scale crouch's foot drift dropped from 5cm to a millimetre.
* **Releasing a chest or hip drag now lands the figure on its feet.** After a big lateral chest drag with steps, or a pull beyond the arm's reach, the figure used to be left standing a few centimetres in the air: the release settle was holding the let-go joint fixed even when it was the trunk, and a foot that stepped beyond its leg's reach had locked the pelvis in place. Trunk gestures now let the body drop onto the planted feet, and a foot can never claim more reach than its leg has.
* **Releasing an IK drag lands the figure exactly.** The animated release settle now finishes with every planted foot exactly on its ground contact — position and flat sole — and the released joint exactly where it was let go, instead of within a 5mm band: a moderate strained pull's release lands its feet within a third of a millimetre (was 5mm). Drag motion itself is unchanged — holding the feet exact mid-drag was measured to mask the balance stepper's strain signal and was rejected.

### Fixed
* **A second figure in the scene can be posed.** Every posing action used to address the first figure loaded; clicking a joint on any figure now makes that figure the posing target, and undo follows the figure each edit belonged to.
* **Delete is back on the context menu after a walk or crouch.** Right-click picked the model by its rest-pose bounds, which a full-body IK walk had left behind; a posed figure is now picked by where its joints actually are.
* **Grabbing the head no longer makes it tremble.** Clicking a head picks one of its face joints, and the drag target for such a grab was following the head's rotation; the neck turns the head with every move, so the target chased its own motion and the head shook. A head grab now keeps a fixed grab offset (the rotating offset stays for finger grabs on a hand, where it is needed).
* **Linux build with GCC/clang.** `FabrikSolver::Settings` — a nested struct with default member initializers — was used as a `= {}` default argument inside its enclosing class, which GCC and clang reject (GCC bug 88165); MSVC accepts it, so the break only showed on Linux. The settings struct now lives at namespace scope with an in-class alias, so every call site is unchanged. (#24 — thanks @DmitryAkDev)
* **Linux: the viewport control strip and the boot splash appeared as a second window, stacked behind the main window.** Both overlays are owned top-level windows (the native Vulkan viewport composites over ordinary child widgets) but were created with the full application-window type, which X11/Wayland window managers list in the taskbar and stack as they please. They are now tool windows — kept out of the window list and above their owner on every platform — and the strip re-asserts itself whenever the main window is activated. (#25 — thanks @DmitryAkDev)
* **Fresh installs since 0.3.6 were missing the `Preferences` table** ("no such table: Preferences" at startup). The schema file is executed by a split-on-semicolon loader, and a header comment added in 0.3.6 contained a semicolon that corrupted the first statement. The comment is reworded, and a startup repair recreates the table on already-affected databases — no Factory Reset needed. (#26 — thanks @DmitryAkDev)
* **Figure import on Linux/macOS: mixed-case content paths now resolve.** Figure files reference content with different letter case than the folders on disk, which NTFS absorbs but case-sensitive filesystems reject — so every import there failed with "cannot resolve base geometry". The resolver now tries the exact-case path first and falls back to a per-component case-insensitive match. (#27 — thanks @DmitryAkDev)

### Added

Joint buffers are now per frame in flight.

The key light relies on its real shadow map instead.

The per-region **roughness, specular-mask, translucency, and micro-detail (pore) normal maps** are all sampled now instead of reduced to scalars, over import-baked **UV tangents** (with the screen-space fallback retained).
* **`.exr` environment support.** OpenEXR panoramas work everywhere `.hdr` does — the environments folder, its subfolders, the thumbnail menu, and the `<appDir>` override — decoded via tinyexr (whose ZIP inflate reuses the miniz already in the build; no new system dependency).
* **HDRI menu folder categories.** Panoramas can be organized into subfolders of the `hdri/` library folder; the thumbnail menu groups them under a heading per subfolder (bright title, spacing, and a hairline rule), with uncategorized files listed first. * **The wet-plastic skin fix.** Skin materials disable their glossy lobe and author the real response in dual-lobe specular — the importer used to read the disabled lobe's zero roughness, rendering skin mirror-smooth. Materials are now interpreted through their active specular **lobes** (dual-lobe → glossy → defaults), with reflectivity mapped to true F0 (skin lands at its real ~2–3%), map-carried channels handled honestly, a hard 4%-dielectric F0 cap, and a horizon fade that stops bump-bent normals reflecting environment light from under the surface. Previously the GPU redrew at full swap rate even when idle, and the vsynced present throttled the whole GUI thread, making the entire app feel sluggish; the viewport now idles at zero GPU cost. A follow-up frame is self-scheduled only when the swapchain was just rebuilt. Un-obscuring the window also no longer forces a needless swapchain rebuild (only a real size change does).
* **Image-based-lighting bakes are multi-threaded.** The GGX specular prefilter and the BRDF-LUT integration (millions of samples, previously single-threaded) now run across all CPU cores — removing the multi-second startup freeze on first viewport expose and making Environment-panel HDRI switches several times faster. (New shared helper: `rendering/parallelfor.h`, used by every CPU bake.)
* **Faster figure import.** The per-corrective Catmull-Clark delta-field subdivision (~100+ full-mesh stencil passes, the dominant slice of subdivision's import cost) is parallelized across cores, and the skin-weight blending in subdivision no longer allocates a hash map per cage face/edge (~50k calls per import). Texture decode also got cheaper (see the Asset Manager note — the same decode path serves imports).
* **Snappier Asset Manager.** Thumbnails are now decoded *at* thumbnail size (letting the JPEG codec do the reduction) instead of decoding full-size images on the GUI thread and scaling down — eliminating browse-jank in folders with large preview images. The directory tree also stops rebuilding its state icons from resources on every repaint, and posing no longer allocates per mouse-move in the skinning update.

### Fixed
* **White dots in the centers of the eyes.** Material *colours* dialed by a preset were ignored in favour of the channel defaults (`current_value` vs. `value`) — so characters that dial their pupil diffuse to black rendered a light-grey pupil disc that read as a bright white dot. Dialed colours are now honoured; as a side effect **all figure textures render at their true brightness** (they were previously multiplied by a 75%-grey default tint).
* **White seam lines along UV boundaries when zoomed out.** Character textures leave the space between UV islands white with no padding, so the GPU mip chain averaged that background through every texture seam at viewing distance (clean when zoomed in, worse zoomed out). Decoded textures are now gutter-filled at import: the mesh's own UV coverage is rasterized and the unused background flooded with colours dilated from the islands (a pull-push fill, applied to diffuse and normal/bump maps alike), so every mip averages island colour into island colour. Covered texels are untouched — close-up rendering is byte-identical.
* **The mid-forearm joint no longer bends.** The figure format marks unposeable rotation channels with a `locked` flag (distinct from `clamped` limits); the importer now honours it, pinning those axes at rest. This locks the anatomically impossible bend at the mid-limb twist joints — most visibly the mid-forearm joint on both arms — while keeping their natural axial twist free within its authored range. The old name-based twist-bone heuristic missed this joint (its name doesn't say "twist" in most figure generations) and is now only a fallback for old figures that predate the `locked` flag.
* **Posing-overlay flicker.** The skeleton/gizmo overlay's vertex buffer was rewritten by the CPU each frame while the previous in-flight frame could still be reading it on the GPU; it's now one buffer per frame-in-flight.

## [0.3.0] - 2026-08-12
### Added
* **Rigged character figure import — the full pipeline.** PoseStudio can now import posable, morphable character figures from their native scene format (`.duf`/`.dsf` — a gzip-compressed JSON preset that references geometry, rig, skin-weight, morph, and UV data files spread across a content folder) and render them, textured, in bind pose. This is a large new subsystem (`src/viewport/import/figure/`), built as a Qt-free, **format-agnostic reader**: one reader handles many figure families and generations because the format is self-describing — geometry, bones, weights, morphs, and materials all come out of the files, so there is no per-figure branching. **File → Import → `.DUF (DUF File)`** opens it; a figure also loads by **double-clicking it in the Asset Manager grid**, from the command line / "open with", and a companion `.pose` file can be applied the same ways.
  * **Geometry + material zones:** polygons are triangulated and grouped per material zone, with the external UV set applied seam-aware. Physically-based materials carry a base color plus diffuse / normal / bump / roughness maps, and merge the `material_library` base a preset extends (so a preset that only *overrides* channels still resolves correctly). Transparency for the eye's clear shells (cornea / moisture) is keyed on the material's refraction weight, so the iris shows through.
  * **Character-shape morphs:** the preset's dialed control channels are walked through their driver formulas to the real head/body shape morphs, whose sparse deltas are **baked into the base vertices at import** — turning the generic base into the specific character (face + body). The character's overall height (a driven figure scale) is applied too.
  * **Joint-center adjustment:** the same shape morphs also carry driver formulas that shift each bone's rest position, so the **skeleton follows the character's new proportions** — posed limbs pivot at the right places and the joint gizmo sits on the body.
  * **Skeleton + GPU skinning:** the bone hierarchy and per-vertex top-4 skin weights are parsed and skinned on the GPU (linear-blend skinning through a per-model joint-matrix storage buffer). Posing is anatomically correct — each joint rotates in its own oriented frame and rotation order, so elbows/knees/shoulders bend about their true axes.
  * **Joint-driven pose correctives:** corrective morphs that fire as a function of bend angle to fix mesh crumpling ("candy-wrapper") at deep elbow/knee/shoulder/hip bends. They are discovered *structurally* (a morph driven by a joint rotation channel), so they work across figure generations regardless of file naming; each corrective's driver formula is translated into a small stack-program and re-evaluated from the live pose, re-morphing the affected mesh on the fly.
  * **Catmull-Clark subdivision:** the low-resolution base cage is smoothed into the render mesh (positions smoothed, UVs seam-preserving, skin weights blended) so limbs/torso/face read round rather than faceted. Because subdivision is a linear operator, each corrective's sparse deltas ride onto the dense mesh through the same stencils, so the runtime re-morph is unchanged.
  * **Skin shading:** cheap subsurface skin shading (wrap-lighting + a warm subsurface band), tangent-space normal-map support (a screen-space cotangent frame, no vertex tangents), and grayscale bump maps applied as a mip-stable texture-space perturbation — which is what gives the skin its surface grain.
  * **Build dependencies:** two more header-only libraries are fetched via `FetchContent` at pinned tags — **nlohmann/json** (JSON parsing, deliberately kept out of Qt so the importer core stays Qt-free) and **miniz** (gzip inflate for the compressed scene files). CMake now also enables the **C** language for miniz's C implementation sources.
* **Interactive posing UI.** An imported figure is posable directly in the viewport:
  * **Click-to-pose:** a left-click that lands on a joint selects it and enters posing (the drag rotates that joint); a click on empty space still orbits the camera.
  * **Rotate gizmo:** three axis rings (X/Y/Z) around the selected joint, drawn in the joint's own rotation frame; dragging a ring performs an axis-constrained rotation (including twist).
  * **Joint rotation limits:** each joint is clamped to its anatomical range of motion (read from the figure's per-axis rotation limits), so bends stay realistic — a knee can't hyper-extend, the neck won't over-rotate, etc.
  * **Pose undo / redo** (`Ctrl+Z` / `Ctrl+Y`) over committed pose edits.
  * **Pose save / load:** **File → Save Pose… / Load Pose…** round-trip the figure's joint rotations through a small plain-text `.pose` file; a `.pose` passed on the command line / "open with" applies onto the imported figure.
* **Viewport shader modes (a "shader picker").** A floating dropdown in the viewport's top-right corner switches between **twelve** shade modes instantly, with no pipeline swap: Rendered (skin/subsurface), **PBR** (physically-based — the photoreal mode), three Matcaps (Studio / Skin / Metal), Toon / Cel, Clay, Lighting-Only, Flat Shaded, Normals, Albedo (unlit), and UV Checker. The selector is a real menu, so it matches the app's menu styling exactly.
* **Anti-aliasing (MSAA).** The viewport renders with multisample anti-aliasing (up to 4×, device-dependent, with a single-sample fallback), so figure and model silhouettes are smooth instead of jagged.
* **Three-point studio lighting.** The scene is lit by a proper **key / fill / back (rim)** rig, world-fixed relative to the subject so lighting stays consistent as the camera orbits, giving imported figures dimensional, studio-portrait lighting. The PBR mode evaluates a full Cook-Torrance BRDF per light plus a hemisphere image-based ambient, a subsurface term for skin, and a fresnel back-rim, ACES-tonemapped.

### Changed
* **Content-folder resolution for figures.** The figure importer resolves a preset's cross-file references against the content-root folder(s): the folder auto-detected from the imported file's own location, **unioned** with any roots the user has configured. When a figure is browsed from a presets-only folder (with no co-located `data/` folder), the interactive import now offers a **"Locate Content Folder…"** prompt, remembers the chosen root, and retries — so those figures import instead of failing silently. A file that isn't a figure (pose / material / light presets share the extension) reports a clear message rather than doing nothing.
* **Skeleton overlay hidden by default.** Importing a figure no longer draws the bone cage over the character; the rotate gizmo is the visual affordance. Joints stay clickable (picking is independent of what's drawn), so a click on a joint still selects it and brings up the gizmo.
* **Menu styling refresh.** The menu-bar dropdown menus (and the viewport shader menu, which is itself a real menu) now share the Asset Manager's dark surface (`#252627`) and blue hover highlight (`#314D7A`), with rounded corners and no drop shadow.
* **Default viewport lighting upgraded** from a single directional key light to the three-point rig above; the mesh fragment shading (both the default Rendered mode and PBR) was reworked to use it.

## [0.2.0] - 2026-06-24
### Added
* **3D Viewport (Vulkan):** Introduced the foundation of a real, Vulkan-backed 3D viewport (`src/viewport/`), replacing the static dark placeholder widget in the main window. The viewport hosts a custom `QWindow` (not Qt's `QVulkanWindow`) so the app owns the device, swapchain, and render passes directly, and renders an infinite floor grid plus imported meshes (see below) through a real orbit camera with mouse/wheel navigation.
  * **Qt / engine boundary:** The subsystem is split into a thin Qt-facing layer (`ViewportWidget`, `VulkanWindow` — the only files allowed to include Qt) and a Qt-free rendering core under `rendering/` and `scene/` (pure Vulkan + std + GLM), keeping the engine reusable and testable.
  * **Layered Vulkan lifetime:** `VulkanContext` (instance/device/queues/VMA allocator) is created once; `VulkanSwapchain` (images, shared depth buffer, render pass, framebuffers) is the resolution-dependent layer rebuilt on resize; `VulkanRenderer` owns per-frame state and drives acquire → record → submit → present, with coalesced resize handling.
  * **Infinite floor grid:** Drawn as a single full-screen quad whose fragment shader reconstructs the world point on the `y=0` plane per pixel, with anti-aliased lines via `fwidth` derivatives, distance fade, and correct `gl_FragDepth` so scene geometry occludes it (`scene/grid.{h,cpp}` + `grid.vert/frag`).
  * **Orbit camera:** A pure-GLM orbit `Camera` with Vulkan-correct clip-space conventions (`scene/camera.{h,cpp}`). Drags drive the camera only when the press began inside the viewport, so opening a modal dialog (e.g. the Import file picker) over the native child window can't leak a button-held move and snap the view.
  * **Graceful degradation:** If `QVulkanInstance::create()` fails, the viewport falls back to an inline message instead of preventing the app from launching.
* **Viewport object context menu (right-click → Delete):** Right-clicking a model in the 3D viewport now picks the object under the cursor (a camera ray vs. each model's bounding box) and opens a context menu; its first action, **Delete**, removes that model from the scene. Deletion waits for the GPU to go idle before freeing the model's buffers/textures so it's safe mid-session. Picking is bounding-box level for now (fine for selecting whole imported objects).
* **Import menu:** **File → Import** is now a submenu enumerating the planned model formats — `.ABC`, `.BVH`, `.DAE`, `.FBX`, `.GLB`, `.OBJ`, `.PLY`, `.STL`, `.USD` — with only `.OBJ (Wavefront)` enabled today; the rest are disabled placeholders that will light up as each importer lands.
* **OBJ model import + textured mesh rendering path:** The first working importer — **File → Import → `.OBJ (Wavefront)`** opens a file dialog and loads the model into the 3D scene, lit by a directional key light + ambient fill, with its **diffuse texture (MTL `map_Kd`)** mapped on and tinted by `Kd` (geometry with no texture/material falls back to a light neutral grey, `#D1D1D1`), sitting correctly on the floor grid with depth. This stood up the entire mesh-rendering path the viewport previously lacked:
  * **GPU resources:** `VulkanBuffer` (`rendering/vulkanbuffer.{h,cpp}`) and `VulkanTexture` (`rendering/vulkanimage.{h,cpp}`, an sRGB sampled image with a generated mip chain) — move-only VMA RAII wrappers — plus `submitImmediate` (`rendering/vulkancommands.{h,cpp}`), the shared one-shot graphics-queue submit used for every load-time upload/transition.
  * **Lit, textured mesh pipeline:** A `Scene` (`scene/scene.{h,cpp}`) owning the mesh pipeline, a per-frame camera/lighting UBO (descriptor set 0), a shared set-1 layout + 1×1 white fallback texture, and the imported `Model` list; an interleaved `Vertex` (pos/normal/uv) format; per-draw push constants (`model` + `baseColor`); per-material diffuse texture in set 1 (`albedo = baseColor * texture`). `PipelineConfig` gained vertex-input, descriptor-set-layout, and cull-mode knobs. Two-sided shading (normal flipped per `gl_FrontFacing`) so inconsistent OBJ winding still reads correctly.
  * **Codec stays out of the core:** the Qt-free `loadObj()` (`scene/objloader.{h,cpp}`, over tinyobjloader) de-indexes geometry, generates smooth normals when absent, groups by material, and resolves `map_Kd` paths (including the quoted, space-containing absolute paths some exporters emit); the **Qt layer decodes the image files via `QImage`** and hands raw RGBA8 down — so the renderer needs no image-codec dependency yet supports every format Qt's plugins do. A texture that can't be found or decoded logs a warning and falls back to the base color rather than failing the import.
  * **Command-line / "open with":** any `.obj` path passed as a launch argument is imported on startup (queued until the renderer is ready), so the app works as a drag-onto-exe / file-association target too.
  * **Import progress indicator:** interactive imports show a modal, staged progress dialog (reading file → decoding textures → uploading to GPU), shown immediately and kept up for the whole import so large models no longer freeze the window with no feedback. The work is still synchronous on the GUI thread; the dialog repaints between phases.
  * **Faster import (batched GPU uploads):** a model's mesh buffers and textures are now uploaded through a single batched command submission (`ImmediateBatch`) instead of a separate submit + GPU fence-wait per buffer and per texture (~3 blocking round-trips per material group). On models with many material groups this is the dominant import cost, so this is a large speed-up. Trade-off: staging buffers for the whole model are held in memory until the one submit completes.
  * Non-diffuse PBR maps (normal/metal/rough) are a later step — OBJ doesn't natively carry them.
* **Build: Vulkan, GLM, VMA & tinyobjloader dependencies:** `CMakeLists.txt` now requires the LunarG Vulkan SDK (`find_package(Vulkan REQUIRED)` plus a check for the `glslc` compiler) and fetches GLM (`1.0.1`), AMD's Vulkan Memory Allocator (`v3.1.0`), and tinyobjloader (`v2.0.0rc13`) header-only via `FetchContent` at pinned tags — a fresh clone needs only Qt + the Vulkan SDK, nothing vendored. GLSL shaders under `src/viewport/shaders/` are compiled to SPIR-V via `glslc` and mirrored next to the executable on every build. CMake minimum raised to 3.21.
